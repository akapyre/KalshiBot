# A New Review Just Put GLP-1s And Bone Health On The Radar

There's a new review making rounds on GLP-1 drugs and bone health, and I think most of you guys are going to scroll past it because "bone" sounds like a problem for your grandma. It isn't. If you're dropping serious weight on sema, tirz or reta, your skeleton is part of the lean mass conversation whether you planned for it or not. So let's talk about it.

First, the honest part: the coverage I have in front of me doesn't give me the hard numbers from that review, so I'm NOT going to pretend it does and quote you effect sizes that don't exist. What I can do is explain exactly why bone keeps showing up in GLP-1 research and what the mechanisms actually are.

WHY BONE IS EVEN IN THIS CONVERSATION

Bone isn't a dead rock holding you up. It's living tissue that's constantly being torn up and repaved. Osteoclasts chew up old bone, osteoblasts lay down new bone. Think of a road crew working 24/7. When demolition outpaces repaving, density drops.

Now here's the part lifters should care about:

1. Bone mass tracks body mass.
Your skeleton builds to the load it's asked to carry. Mechanical loading is the signal. Drop 20% of your bodyweight and the signal your bones have been responding to for years just got a lot quieter.

2. Energy deficit is a construction problem.
Building bone is expensive. It needs calories, protein, calcium and vitamin D. On a GLP-1, appetite is suppressed, total intake falls, and nutrient-dense food often falls with it. You can't repave the road if nobody delivers the bricks.

3. Muscle loss and bone loss travel together.
Muscle pulls on bone. That's part of the loading signal. Lose a big chunk of lean mass and you lose both the pull and the balance/stability that keeps older people from falling in the first place.

4. The GIP question.
GIP receptors aren't just in your pancreas. GIP has long been studied for its effects on bone turnover, which is why tirzepatide (GLP-1 + GIP) is an interesting case compared to a pure GLP-1 like semaglutide. Whether that difference actually shows up in human bone density is an open question, not a settled one.

WHAT WE STILL DON'T KNOW

* Most of the big weight loss trials measured weight on a scale, not bone mineral density on a DXA.
* Fractures are rare events. You need huge numbers and years of follow-up to see a real signal, and a lot of these drugs haven't been out long enough in enough people.
* Almost all of the data comes from older, diabetic or obese populations. That is NOT the same as a 32-year-old lifter running a GLP-1 to get lean.
* Nobody has studied what resistance training plus high protein does to that bone picture on a GLP-1, which is frustrating, because that's exactly the population reading this.
* There is essentially nothing on retatrutide here.

THE PART YOU CONTROL

The stuff that protects bone during weight loss is the same boring stuff that protects muscle. Lift heavy and keep loading the skeleton. Keep protein high. Don't let calcium and vitamin D fall off a cliff just because you're eating 1,200 calories you don't feel like finishing. And rate of loss matters, faster isn't automatically better.

If you're over 50, post-menopausal, or you've already got a fracture history, a baseline DXA before a big cut is a genuinely smart ask of your doctor. That's the one group where I'd actually push on this.

This is not something most of you need to be panicking about. It IS something I want on your radar before you drop 60 pounds and never once think about what's underneath the muscle.
