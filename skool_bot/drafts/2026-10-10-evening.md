# How To Build A Log That Actually Tells You Something

One of the most common things I get asked after someone starts a new compound is "how do I know if it's actually working?" And almost every time, the answer is that they have no data to look at. Just vibes and a vague memory of how they felt three weeks ago. So let's talk about how to build a log you'll actually use.

1. Take a baseline BEFORE you start anything.

This is the step everyone skips and the one that makes the whole thing worthless. You cannot measure change from a starting point you never wrote down. Give yourself a week of normal life first and record it. Weight, waist, resting heart rate, sleep, top sets on your main lifts, how you feel. Boring week, real numbers.

2. Pick 2 or 3 primary outcomes. That's it.

If everything is important, nothing is. Decide up front what would actually count as this working for you.

* Fat loss? Scale trend + waist + progress photos.
* Recovery? Soreness score + session RPE + sleep quality.
* Focus/energy? A 1-10 rating at the same time every day.
* Joint/tendon stuff? Pain score during a specific movement, not "overall."

Everything else is a secondary note, not a headline.

3. Measure under the SAME conditions every time.

Same time of day, same clothes, same scale, same spot on the waist, same lighting for photos. Most of what people call a "result" in week two is just measurement noise. Standardize the conditions and the noise shrinks dramatically.

4. Change ONE thing at a time.

This is the big one. If you start a new compound, a new training split, and a new diet in the same week, you've learned nothing. You've got three variables and one outcome. When something goes great you won't know what to repeat, and when something goes sideways you won't know what to stop.

5. Log subjective stuff with numbers, not adjectives.

"Felt kinda tired" is useless in six weeks. A 1-10 energy score, a 1-10 sleep score, and a 1-10 appetite score take ten seconds and give you a line you can actually graph. Your memory will lie to you. A number from that morning won't.

6. Timestamp side effects.

When something feels off, write down what it was, how bad (1-10), what time it hit, and what you'd eaten or done that day. Patterns show up fast this way. Half the things people blame on a compound turn out to be dehydration, under-eating, or four hours of sleep, and the log is what tells you which one it was.

7. Get bloodwork on both ends.

Before and after beats "after" alone every single time. A panel pulled mid-run with nothing to compare it to tells you almost nothing about what changed. Pull it fasted, same lab, same-ish time of day, and keep the PDFs somewhere you'll find them in a year.

8. Review on a schedule. Do NOT review daily.

Daily weigh-ins are fine, daily ANALYSIS is where people lose their minds. Log every day, look at the trend every 2 or 4 weeks. You're looking for the direction of the line, not yesterday's dot.

9. Write down the confounders.

Alcohol, travel, sickness, a brutal work week, your period, a salty dinner, a missed dose. One line is enough. When week five looks weird, the explanation is usually already sitting in your notes.

10. Be honest about novelty.

The first two weeks of anything new feel great because it's new. You're also training harder, sleeping better, and paying attention. That's not a reason to be cynical, it's a reason to keep logging past week four, which is where the real signal lives.

Tool doesn't matter. Notes app, spreadsheet, a notebook in your gym bag, whatever you'll actually open. The compound is only as useful as your ability to tell whether it did anything, and that comes down to whether you wrote it down.
