# CagriSema vs Semaglutide 2.4: The Meta-Analysis Numbers Are In

You've probably seen CagriSema thrown around as "the next big one" for the past two years, mostly on hype and single-trial headlines. A new systematic review and meta-analysis just pooled the randomized data head-to-head against semaglutide 2.4mg, so let's talk about what it actually found.

WHAT CAGRISEMA EVEN IS

It's a fixed-dose combination of cagrilintide and semaglutide. Semaglutide you know. Cagrilintide is a long-acting amylin analog. Amylin is a hormone your beta cells release alongside insulin, and it works on a different set of brakes than GLP-1 does.

Simple version: GLP-1 turns down the gas pedal on appetite through the gut-brain axis. Amylin is working on a separate circuit in the hindbrain that tells you the meal is over, and it also appears to improve how well your brain listens to leptin. Two different levers on the same machine. That's the whole thesis behind the combo.

WHAT THE STUDY DID

This was a PRISMA-compliant review, pre-registered on PROSPERO, searching PubMed, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, ScienceDirect, ClinicalTrials.gov and WHO ICTRP through June 30, 2026. Inclusion was randomized controlled trials comparing CagriSema to semaglutide 2.4mg or placebo in adults with overweight or obesity, with or without type 2 diabetes.

Five trials, 4,746 unique participants. These are HUMANS, and randomized humans at that, which puts this way up the evidence ladder from most of what we talk about here.

WHAT THEY FOUND (vs semaglutide 2.4mg)

* Weight loss: an extra -6.89 percentage points (95% CI -8.70 to -5.06)
* Absolute body weight: -7.16 kg more (95% CI -9.15 to -5.17)
* BMI: -2.41 kg/m² more
* Hitting 20% or more total weight loss: roughly DOUBLE the likelihood (RR 2.10, 95% CI 1.78 to 2.47)
* Waist circumference: -4.07 cm more

That's not a rounding error. Nearly 7 extra kilos on top of what is already the reference drug in this category.

THE PART NOBODY POSTS

No incremental glycemic benefit. HbA1c difference was -0.07 (95% CI -0.14 to 0.01), and fasting glucose landed entirely inside the prespecified minimal important difference. Translation: adding the amylin analog made people lose more weight, it did NOT meaningfully move blood sugar beyond what semaglutide already does.

Side effects: injection site reactions were over 3x more frequent (RR 3.14, 95% CI 1.42 to 6.96). GI adverse events were not significantly increased under the primary model (RR 1.09, 95% CI 0.97 to 1.21), which honestly surprised me given how much more total appetite suppression is happening.

WHAT WE STILL DON'T KNOW

The authors say it straight. The evidence is insufficient to tell whether serious adverse events or neoplasms differ between treatments. Long-term durability is undetermined. Cardiovascular outcomes are undetermined. And BODY COMPOSITION is undetermined.

That last one is the one that matters most to lifters, and it's worth connecting to something else published the same week. A narrative review in Drugs on glucose-lowering therapies and sarcopenia pointed out that muscle outcomes are rarely prespecified in these pivotal trials at all, and that DXA- or bioimpedance-derived "lean mass" doesn't directly quantify contractile muscle and gets influenced by body water shifts during weight loss. Their point: you can't infer meaningful muscle loss from a lean mass number without also measuring strength or physical performance. That cuts both ways, it means the scary lean-mass headlines are overstated AND it means nobody has actually shown us what a drop this aggressive does to a trained person's muscle.

So: more weight off, more waist off, no extra glucose benefit, more injection site irritation, and a giant question mark where the body composition and long-term safety data should be.
