# 8,127 Women On Tirzepatide: 15.5% Body Weight In 6 Months

You've probably seen people argue that the GLP-1 trial numbers only happen in trials, with the free meds, the dietitian calls and the perfect compliance. Nature Medicine just dropped a real-world readout on 8,127 women running a structured telehealth program, and the numbers held up. So let's talk about it.

WHAT THEY ACTUALLY DID

This was an observational study in the UK, not a randomized trial. 8,127 women starting a GLP-1 receptor agonist through a digital-first program. 99.8% of them were on tirzepatide, so this is basically a tirzepatide paper.

The program wasn't just "ship a pen and good luck." It included:

* MANDATORY bloodwork monitoring
* Weekly video-supported follow up
* Clinician-guided dose titration

That structure matters, and I'll come back to it.

WHAT THEY FOUND AT 6 MONTHS

1. Mean weight reduction of 16.1 kg (about 35 lbs), which was 15.5% of starting body weight.
2. 83.1% hit at least 10% weight loss. 22.4% hit at least 20%.
3. Results were consistent across age, baseline weight, and socioeconomic strata. That's one of the more interesting lines in the whole paper, the response didn't fall apart by demographic.
4. In people with paired labs, HbA1c dropped 3.3 mmol/mol. It normalized in 84.5% of those who started in the diabetes range and 96.3% of those in prediabetes range.
5. Lipids moved too: total cholesterol down 0.77 mmol/L, LDL down 0.61, triglycerides down 0.47.
6. Retention was 91.4% at 6 months, with no serious treatment-related adverse events reported.

That retention number is the one I'd highlight. Real-world GLP-1 discontinuation is usually where the story falls apart. 91.4% sticking around at 6 months with weekly check-ins and required labs tells you the wrapper around the compound is doing real work.

WHAT WE STILL DON'T KNOW

The authors say this themselves, so I'm not reaching here:

* Observational design, NO comparator group. There's no "same women, no drug" arm to compare against.
* Self-pay population. People who pay out of pocket and show up to weekly video calls are not a random sample of humans.
* Only 6 months of follow-up. Nothing here tells you about maintenance or regain.
* No body composition data. Weight is weight. We don't know how much of that 16.1 kg was fat vs lean tissue.

That last one lines up with a narrative review that came out the same week in Nutrients on GLP-1s and skeletal muscle. Their conclusion was blunt: clinical studies consistently show bigger reductions in fat mass than in fat-free mass, but "fat-free mass" and "lean mass" are NOT the same thing as skeletal muscle. Direct muscle and functional outcomes were measured in very few studies, results were mixed, and incident sarcopenia was almost never evaluated. Their verdict is that current evidence is insufficient to call the net effect on muscle either way.

So you've got great scale-weight data and still-thin muscle data. That's the honest state of things.

The takeaway I'd pull out of this: the compound works outside of a trial setting, but the people in this paper had labs, weekly contact and guided titration built in. That's the part people skip, and it's probably not the part you want to skip.
