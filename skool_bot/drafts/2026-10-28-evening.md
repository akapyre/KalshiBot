# How To Build Your Off-Ramp BEFORE You Need It

You've probably seen the headlines asking what happens when everyone goes ON and then OFF of GLP-1s. CBC ran a piece on exactly that this week, and I'm not going to pretend to know what their experts concluded, but the question itself is the right one to be asking, so let's talk about it.

Here's the thing nobody wants to hear: the hardest part of a GLP-1 isn't the hunger, the nausea, or the titration. It's the day the appetite signal comes back and you have no structure underneath it. Coverage changes, supply changes, cost changes, life changes. The off-ramp happens to almost everybody eventually. So you build it on the way UP, not on the way down.

Here's how I'd approach that, in order of impact.

1. Build the habits while appetite is suppressed, not after.

This is the whole game. When food noise is quiet, you have the easiest window of your life to install routines. That's when you learn to cook, learn portions, learn what a protein-forward plate actually looks like. Willpower is cheap right now, USE it. If the only reason you're eating less is the compound, you have no off-ramp, you have a cliff.

2. Know your real maintenance intake.

Most people on a GLP-1 have NO idea what they're actually eating, because they're not hungry and they stopped paying attention. Track honestly for a couple weeks at some point. Not forever, just long enough to know your numbers. When appetite returns, that number is your guardrail instead of a guess.

3. Protect the lean mass the entire time.

* Resistance training, consistently, not "when I feel up to it"
* Protein at every meal, since total intake drops hard on these
* Progressive overload, so you're giving the body a reason to keep the muscle

Muscle is your metabolic insurance policy. The more of it you keep on the way down, the less brutal maintenance feels on the way off.

4. Pick your maintenance behaviors, in writing.

Not vibes. Actual rules you can repeat on a bad week. Something like: lift 3x a week, protein target hit daily, steps target, weigh in on the same day. Three to five things. If your plan is "eat healthy and stay active," you don't have a plan, you have a mood.

5. Keep weighing yourself, and define your trigger.

People stop stepping on the scale the second they come off, and then re-engage 25 pounds later. Decide ahead of time what number or what trend means you tighten things back up. A 3 to 5 pound fluctuation is water and life. A steady six-week climb is a signal.

6. Expect the appetite to come back and don't panic.

That return is PHARMACOLOGY, not a character flaw. Your hunger signaling was being modulated, and then it isn't. Hunger coming back is the expected outcome. What you do in the two weeks after is what decides how the next year goes.

7. Have a reason that isn't the scale.

Strength, bloodwork, sleep, how you look in the mirror, joints, energy. People who only ever chased the number tend to quit the second the number stops moving. People with a second reason keep training.

Honestly, the compound was never the hard part. The compound is the easy part. The structure you build underneath it while it's working is what you actually get to keep.
