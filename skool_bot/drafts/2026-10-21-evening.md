# That Purity Number Isn't The Proof You Think It Is

This one might sting a little, but it needs to be said. A lot of you are treating a single testing number like it's a fact of nature, and it is NOT. So let's talk about it.

There was a piece out this week on melanocortin peptide assay reproducibility, and the whole point of it is right there in the title: potency data does not travel between laboratories. Same molecule, different lab, different numbers. That's not a conspiracy, that's just how analytical chemistry works.

WHY NUMBERS DON'T TRAVEL

A potency or purity result isn't a measurement of truth, it's a measurement of a sample, by a method, on a specific day, by a specific lab.

* The method matters. HPLC purity (how much of the peak area is your compound) and a bioassay (does it actually activate the receptor) are answering two COMPLETELY different questions.
* The reference standard matters. If two labs calibrate against different standards, they can both be "right" and still disagree.
* The sample matters. One vial out of a run is not the run.
* Peptides are also just hard. Counter-ions, residual water, salt content, all of it shifts what "100mg" even means on a scale.

Think of it like stepping on a different scale at a different gym. Both say you're a number. Neither one of them is lying. They're just not the same scale.

PURITY IS NOT THE ONLY QUESTION

Here's the part people skip. This week there were also reports of GLP-1 injections being recalled over a "lack of assurance of sterility." Read that again. That's not a potency problem. A product can be chemically beautiful on paper and still have a sterility, endotoxin, or handling problem that no purity percentage would ever catch.

So when someone waves a document at you, ask what question it actually answered:

1. What was tested? The specific batch, or a sample from months ago?
2. What method was used, and against what standard?
3. Does it cover purity ONLY, or sterility and endotoxin too?
4. Who ran it, and is the full report there or just a screenshot of the good part?
5. Does the identity match, not just the number? Mass spec identity is a different line than area percent.

I'm not saying testing is worthless. It's the opposite. Testing is one of the best tools we have, which is exactly why it deserves to be read properly instead of used as a marketing badge. The problem isn't the paper. The problem is people treating one number from one lab as the end of the conversation, then being shocked when a retest somewhere else comes back different.

If you're the person who screenshots a result and never looks at the method, I'm not mad at you. I was there too. Just start asking the second question. "What number?" is beginner level. "How was it measured, and what did it NOT measure?" is where you actually start protecting yourself.
