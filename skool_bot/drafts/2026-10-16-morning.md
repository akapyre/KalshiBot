# HHS Just Pushed A Peptide Compounding Policy To The White House

You've probably seen the headline floating around today: HHS is seeking White House review of peptide compounding policy. No details attached, just the headline, and people are already panicking about peptides "getting banned." So let's talk about what that sentence actually means, because the process matters more than the panic.

WHAT "WHITE HOUSE REVIEW" ACTUALLY IS

This is NOT a ban announcement. When HHS (the parent agency over FDA) wants to put out a rule or a formal guidance, it typically has to go through the Office of Management and Budget first for review. That's the step that happens BEFORE the public sees the actual text. So right now, all anyone knows is that something about peptide compounding is sitting in that queue. The headline tells us a policy exists and is moving. It does NOT tell us what the policy says, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

WHY THIS CATEGORY EXISTS IN THE FIRST PLACE

Quick background so the news lands. Compounding pharmacies in the US operate under two sections of the law: 503A (patient-specific compounding) and 503B (outsourcing facilities). For a compounder to legally use a bulk drug substance that isn't an approved drug, that substance generally has to be on an FDA bulk substances list.

FDA sorts nominated substances into categories. Category 1 means it can be used while under evaluation. Category 2 means FDA flagged significant safety risks. A bunch of peptides people in the gym talk about constantly got pushed into that Category 2 bucket back in 2023, which is the single biggest reason the "research chemical" side of this space looks the way it does today. Add in the semaglutide and tirzepatide shortage ending, which shut off mass compounding of those two, and you can see why any new peptide compounding policy gets everyone's attention.

THE OTHER HEADLINE THAT MAKES THIS INTERESTING

Same day, Medscape ran a piece reporting that many people who believe they're on "branded" GLP-1s are actually on compounded product, based on photos. I can't tell you the methodology from the headline alone, and I'm not going to invent numbers. But directionally it points at the exact problem regulators keep circling: a lot of users genuinely do not know what's in the vial they were handed.

WHAT I'D WATCH FOR WHEN THE TEXT DROPS

* Does it touch 503A, 503B, or both? Those are very different reach.
* Does it name specific peptides, or is it a framework for the whole class?
* Does it address GLP-1 compounding specifically, or the broader peptide bulk list?
* Is it a proposed rule (comment period, slow) or guidance (faster)?

WHAT WE STILL DON'T KNOW

Everything that actually matters. Scope, timeline, whether it loosens or tightens. A policy going to review can go either direction, and plenty of them get revised hard before publication. Anyone telling you today exactly what's in it is guessing.

My take: this space was always going to get a real regulatory framework eventually. Clarity is not the enemy here, confusion is. What I don't want is you guys making decisions off a headline with no body text attached.
