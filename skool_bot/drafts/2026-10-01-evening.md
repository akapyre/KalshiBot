# Sermorelin - The Original GH Secretagogue

Sermorelin keeps popping up in articles again, and it's one of those compounds people lump in with "growth hormone" without actually knowing what it does. It is NOT HGH. It's a completely different approach to the same system, so let's talk about it.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS

Sermorelin is GHRH(1-29), the first 29 amino acids of your body's own growth hormone releasing hormone. Natural GHRH is 44 amino acids long, and researchers found that the first 29 carry basically all of the activity. So sermorelin is the trimmed-down, fully active version of a hormone you already make in your hypothalamus.

HISTORY & STATUS

This one has real regulatory history. It was FDA-approved under the brand name Geref, used both as a diagnostic agent to test pituitary function and for pediatric growth hormone deficiency. It was pulled from the US market in the 2000s for business reasons, NOT for safety. Today it mostly lives in the compounding pharmacy and research space, which is why it suddenly feels "new" to people who weren't around for it the first time.

THE MECHANISM (SIMPLE VERSION)

Your pituitary is a factory that makes growth hormone. Injecting HGH is like having a truck pull up and dump finished product on the loading dock, your factory sees the inventory piling up and shuts the line down.

Sermorelin doesn't deliver product. It walks into the office and tells the factory manager to run another shift. It binds the GHRH receptor on the somatotroph cells in your anterior pituitary, which raises cAMP inside the cell and tells it to release the GH it already made.

The important part: the brakes stay on. Somatostatin (your body's "stop making GH" signal) and IGF-1 negative feedback both still work. So you get GH released in PULSES, the way your body naturally does it, rather than a flat elevated line. That's the whole pitch for secretagogues over exogenous GH.

It's also very short-acting, cleared in a matter of minutes, which is exactly why it produces a pulse instead of a plateau.

WHAT PEOPLE REPORT

* Deeper sleep (GH release is heaviest in early slow-wave sleep, so this tracks with the mechanism)
* Better recovery and joint comfort over weeks, not days
* Slow, gradual body composition shifts rather than anything dramatic
* Nothing overnight. This is a "3 months in" compound, not a week one compound

WATCH FOR

* Injection site redness and itching was the most commonly reported reaction in the clinical record
* Flushing or a warm head-rush feeling shortly after administration
* Headache, dizziness
* It depends on a working pituitary. If the pituitary itself is the problem, telling it to work harder does nothing
* Receptor desensitization is a real consideration with constant GHRH signaling, which is why pulsatile patterns were the whole design goal

EVIDENCE LEVEL (BE HONEST HERE)

This is better studied than most of what gets talked about in the peptide space, because it was an actual approved drug with actual pituitary testing data behind it. The mechanism is not speculative, GHRH receptor biology is textbook endocrinology.

Where it gets thin is the stuff people actually want it for. The strong data is in diagnostics and pediatric GH deficiency. Data on healthy adult lifters chasing recovery, sleep, and body composition is MUCH weaker and leans heavily on mechanism plus anecdote. A lot of what you read online about sermorelin for "anti-aging" is marketing built on top of a real mechanism, and those are two different things.

So sermorelin isn't a shortcut to HGH results, and anyone selling it as that is lying to you. It's a nudge to a system you already own, with your body's own safety brakes left intact. That's the trade: smaller ceiling, more physiological.
