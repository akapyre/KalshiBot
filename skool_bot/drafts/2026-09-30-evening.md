# Titration Explained: Why Dose 4 Lands On Top Of Dose 3

Every time a new GLP-1 dosing schedule gets talked about, the titration questions start flying. Lilly just broke down retatrutide dosing data from TRIUMPH-2 at EASD this week, and the first thing people ask is always some version of "why do they go up so slowly?" So let's talk about what titration actually IS mechanically, because once you understand it, the whole schedule makes sense.

THE BUCKET ANALOGY

Picture a bucket with a hole in the bottom. Every dose is you pouring water in. The hole is your body clearing the drug out. Half-life is just how long it takes for half the water to drain.

Here's the part people miss: if you pour again BEFORE the bucket empties, the new water stacks on top of what's left. You're not starting from zero. You're starting from whatever didn't drain yet.

Semaglutide has a half-life around a week. Tirzepatide is around 5 days. Both are dosed weekly. So by design, you are always pouring into a bucket that still has water in it.

STEADY STATE

This is the concept nobody explains simply. Eventually the bucket fills to a level where the amount draining out per week equals the amount you pour in. That's steady state. The level stops climbing and just plateaus.

The rule of thumb in pharmacology is roughly 4 to 5 half-lives to get there. For a weekly GLP-1, that's about a MONTH at any given dose before the level in your blood actually settles.

Read that again, because it explains almost everything:

* Week 1 at a new dose is NOT what that dose feels like
* Week 4 at that same dose is a higher blood level than week 1, with no change in what you injected
* This is why sides can show up 3 weeks into a dose you "tolerated fine"

SO WHY TITRATE AT ALL

Two reasons, and they're different.

1. Receptor adaptation. GLP-1 receptors in the gut and brainstem drive nausea, delayed gastric emptying, and appetite suppression. Ramping lets the nausea side of that response blunt over time while the appetite side sticks around. Go too fast and you get the full gut response at once.

2. Pharmacokinetics. You physically cannot know what a dose does until it's at steady state. Jumping up before then is like adding 40lbs to the bar before you've finished the warmup set. You didn't test the weight, you just guessed.

THE GYM VERSION

Titration is progressive overload. Nobody walks in and loads their 1RM cold. You add a plate, you see how the set moves, you add again. The trials that set these schedules are doing the exact same thing at a population level, finding the point where the benefit is there and the drop-off rate isn't.

And this isn't GLP-1 only. Half-life and steady state apply to everything you run. Short half-life daily peptides hit steady state in a couple days. Long-acting stuff takes weeks. Same math, different timeline.

One thing to be straight about: the TRIUMPH-2 presentation is manufacturer trial data, and retatrutide is not approved. Trial titration schedules are built inside a monitored study, they aren't a menu.

If titration felt like pointless waiting before, hopefully the bucket makes it click. You're not stalling, you're letting the level settle so you actually know what you're working with. Drop your questions below.
