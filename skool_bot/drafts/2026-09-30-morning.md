# Amylin Is The Next Big Lever: Lilly's EloraTZP Beat Tirzepatide 15mg

You've probably seen "amylin" popping up everywhere in the obesity drug headlines the past few months and wondered why anyone cares when tirzepatide already exists. Well, Lilly just dropped mid-stage data on a combo of eloralintide (an amylin receptor agonist) plus tirzepatide, and it reportedly beat tirzepatide 15 mg on BOTH weight loss and A1C in adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes. It's moving to Phase 3. So let's talk about it.

WHAT AMYLIN ACTUALLY IS

Amylin is a hormone your pancreatic beta cells release right alongside insulin every time you eat. It's not a GLP-1. It's a completely separate satiety signal that acts mostly on the area postrema in your brainstem, and it does three things: slows gastric emptying, blunts glucagon after a meal, and tells your brain "we're full, stop."

Think of it like this. GLP-1 is the faucet handle turning down appetite. Amylin is a second, separate handle on a different pipe. Turn both down at once and you get more effect than maxing out either one alone. That's the entire thesis behind these combos.

This isn't a brand new idea either. Pramlintide (Symlin) is an amylin analog that's been FDA-approved since 2005, it just had a rough dosing schedule and never took off for weight. The new generation is long-acting, which changes everything.

WHAT THE TRIAL DID

This was a mid-stage (Phase 2) human trial in adults with obesity AND type 2 diabetes. That population matters, because diabetics historically lose LESS weight on these drugs than non-diabetics do. The comparator wasn't placebo, it was tirzepatide 15 mg, which is the top approved dose of the current best-in-class drug. That's a real benchmark, not a softball.

The reported result: EloraTZP delivered greater weight loss and greater A1C reduction than tirzepatide 15 mg alone. Lilly is pushing the amylin-GIP/GLP-1 combo into Phase 3 off the back of it.

WHAT WE DON'T KNOW YET

I'm going to be straight with you, because the press releases are ahead of the data here.

* The full percentage numbers, trial duration, and dropout rates aren't in what's been released so far. I'm NOT going to guess them for you.
* Tolerability is the big question. Amylin analogs historically bring nausea and vomiting, and you're stacking that on top of a GIP/GLP-1 that already causes GI sides. "More weight loss" means nothing if half the arm can't tolerate it.
* Body composition. This is the one I actually care about as a lifter. Nobody has shown me lean mass data on the amylin combos yet. In animal work amylin signaling is supposed to favor fat loss, but supposed to and proven are two very different things.
* Phase 2 to Phase 3 is where a lot of "astonishing" results get smaller.

ALSO ON MY RADAR THIS WEEK

Lilly also put out full Phase 3 retatrutide data in obesity with type 2 diabetes. Two different outlets are reporting the top-line as "up to 20.8%" and "up to 18.8%" weight loss, which almost certainly means they're quoting different analysis sets or different doses. I'll break the actual paper down once I've read the full readout instead of the headlines.

The bigger picture: the GLP-1 era is turning into the multi-receptor era. GLP-1, GIP, glucagon, and now amylin. Four handles instead of one. If you're researching this space, amylin is the pathway to start reading up on now, because it's going to be in everything within two years.
