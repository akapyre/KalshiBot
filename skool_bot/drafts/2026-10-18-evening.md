# GH Pulses Explained: Your Body Trains Growth Hormone In SETS

One of the questions I get constantly is some version of "why do growth hormone peptides work differently than just taking GH?" And the answer lives entirely in one word: PULSES. So let's talk about it.

Here's the analogy. Your pituitary doesn't drip growth hormone out all day like a slow IV. It trains. It does SETS. A big burst, then a rest period, then another burst. The rest between sets isn't wasted time, it's the whole reason the next set works.

THE THREE PLAYERS

* GHRH — the coach yelling "GO." This is the signal from your hypothalamus telling the pituitary to fire off a pulse.
* Somatostatin — the hand on your chest saying "not yet." This is the brake. When somatostatin is high, GHRH can scream all it wants and you get very little out.
* Ghrelin (and the GHS-R receptor) — the hype man. It amplifies the pulse AND pushes the brake out of the way at the same time.

So a GH pulse is really a tug of war. Gas on, brake off, big set. Gas on, brake still on, weak set.

WHY THE REST BETWEEN SETS MATTERS

GH itself has a very short half-life in your blood, on the order of minutes. That's not a flaw, that's the design. Your liver and tissues are built to read the SHAPE of the signal, not just the amount. Peaks and troughs. If you flatten that out and just hold GH high constantly, the receptors downstream stop reading it the same way, and your own feedback system (IGF-1 and somatostatin climbing) starts hitting the brake harder.

Think about it like training. Ten hard sets with real rest hits different than holding a dumbbell at 50% forever. Same "volume," totally different signal.

WHERE THE COMPOUND CLASSES FIT

1. GHRH analogs (sermorelin, CJC-1295, tesamorelin). These are the coach. They work on the pulse you were already going to have and make it BIGGER. They don't create pulses out of nowhere, which is why somatostatin still limits them.

2. Ghrelin mimetics / GHS-R agonists (ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6, hexarelin, MK-677). These are the hype man. They stimulate release and blunt somatostatin, so the brake comes off too.

3. Exogenous HGH. This skips the whole conversation. You're not amplifying a pulse, you're setting the floor. That's why the response profile, the feedback, and the side effect picture are all different animals.

THE PART PEOPLE SKIP

Your biggest natural GH pulse happens in deep slow-wave sleep, early in the night. If your sleep is garbage, you are nuking the single biggest set of the day and then trying to fix it with a signaling molecule. Training hard and your overall metabolic state move this needle too.

It's the same reason I keep saying fix the foundation first. A secretagogue turns UP a system. It doesn't build you one.
