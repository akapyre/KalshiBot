# What I'm Watching This Week: FDA Pokes At Peptides, A Generic GLP-1, and Nails

Slow news day for actual studies, but three things crossed my desk this morning that matter more to you guys than another "tirzepatide beats semaglutide" rewrite. So let's talk about them.

1. FDA STAFF QUESTIONING THE SAFETY OF "POPULAR PEPTIDES"

This is the big one. Hims & Hers stock slipped overnight after FDA staff raised safety questions about popular peptides, with at least one analyst saying "this isn't the end."

Here's the honest part: the headline doesn't tell us WHICH peptides, what the staff actually said, or whether this is a formal action or just commentary in a review document. I'm not going to guess, and you shouldn't let anyone else guess for you either.

What I can tell you is the background, because this is how these things usually play out. The FDA keeps lists that decide what compounding pharmacies are allowed to make. Substances the agency has flagged for safety or characterization concerns get pushed into the "significant safety risk" bucket, and once that happens, the legal telehealth and compounding supply dries up almost overnight. That's exactly what happened to a bunch of peptides people were buying through clinics a couple years back.

So when FDA staff start publicly questioning peptide safety and a telehealth stock moves on it, the thing being priced in is ACCESS, not necessarily danger. Watch for the actual document before you panic in either direction.

2. TEVA LAUNCHING A GENERIC GLP-1 FOR WEIGHT LOSS

Teva is reportedly introducing the first generic GLP-1 for weight loss. The headline doesn't name the molecule, so I won't either.

But here's the logic: semaglutide and tirzepatide are both still under patent protection in the US. A true generic right now has to be an OLDER molecule whose exclusivity already ran out. So mentally file this as "cheaper access to an earlier-generation GLP-1," NOT "generic Ozempic is here." Those are very different things, and the comment sections are already conflating them.

Still a big deal long term. The first generic in a class is usually the crack in the dam for pricing.

3. NAIL CHANGES ON GLP-1s

A study is being reported as finding nail trouble over four times more common in GLP-1 users. I don't have the full paper, so I can't tell you the population, the design, or how they defined "nail trouble."

What I'd say mechanistically: nails and hair are both keratin structures fed by fast-dividing cells, and they're some of the first tissues your body deprioritizes when energy and protein intake drop hard. We already talked about GLP-1 hair shedding, and the leading explanation there was telogen effluvium from rapid weight loss, NOT the drug attacking your follicles. Brittle, ridged, slow-growing nails would fit the exact same story: not enough protein, not enough calories, not enough micronutrients, for months.

That's a fixable problem. Eat your protein, don't crash the calories, keep your iron, zinc and biotin intake sane. Nails grow slowly, so if something's off, you're looking at what you did 3 to 6 months ago.

WHAT I'M ACTUALLY WATCHING

The FDA item. Everything else on this list is noise by comparison. If that review turns into a list placement, it changes what's legally available to a LOT of people, and the first you'll hear about it is a clinic emailing you that they're discontinuing something.
