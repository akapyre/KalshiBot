# WHO Just Drew A Line On GLP-1s For Kids

You've probably seen the headlines rolling in today: the WHO put out guidance on obesity in children and adolescents, and the summary everyone is running with is "lifestyle first, GLP-1s not routine." Reuters, Forbes and Health Policy Watch all covered it. So let's talk about what that actually means, because a lot of you are going to see this reposted as "WHO says Ozempic is dangerous" and that's NOT what's happening.

WHAT THE GUIDANCE ACTUALLY IS

This is a guideline document, not a study. No new trial, no new numbers. A panel looks at the existing evidence base and makes recommendations for clinicians worldwide. The headline takeaway from the coverage is that structured lifestyle and behavioral intervention stays the foundation of care for kids, and drugs like semaglutide and tirzepatide are not being recommended as a general first-line tool in that age group. Surgery got addressed too.

I'm not going to quote you specific age cutoffs or recommendation strength, because the news write-ups don't give the full guideline text and I'm not guessing at it. The direction is clear though.

WHY THEY'RE CAUTIOUS (THE PART THAT MATTERS TO YOU)

The argument isn't "these drugs don't work." They obviously work. The argument is about the unknowns, and the unknowns in kids are a bigger deal than in a 35 year old lifter:

* Long-term safety data in a still-growing body basically doesn't exist. The trials are short relative to a human childhood.
* Growth, puberty and bone development all happen in a big caloric and nutritional window. Hard appetite suppression during that window is a real question mark.
* Lean mass. We already know meaningful lean tissue comes off with fat on GLP-1s in adults. In a developing skeleton and muscle base, nobody has mapped what that costs.
* Access and cost. Global guidance has to work in places where a lifetime drug isn't realistic.

THE OTHER HALF OF THE STORY

Two other items hit today that are the same conversation wearing different clothes. News-Medical covered work on structured support slowing weight regain after people stop GLP-1s, and FoodNavigator covered diet as a lever for post-GLP-1 regain. I don't have the full methods in front of me, so I'm not throwing numbers at you, but the theme is loud: the hard problem in this entire category is not getting the weight off. It's what happens on day one after the last dose.

That's the actual logic behind the WHO being conservative with kids. If the behavior and food environment never changes, you've bought time, not an outcome.

WHAT THIS DOESN'T SAY

* It does NOT say GLP-1s are unsafe for adults.
* It does NOT pull or restrict anything. Guidance is not regulation.
* It does NOT say no child should ever get one. Clinical judgment for severe cases still exists.

Honestly, the lesson here applies to every single one of you running one of these compounds. The drug is the lever, the habits are the outcome. Lifting, protein, sleep and a food plan you can actually keep are what make the loss stick when the pen goes in the trash.
