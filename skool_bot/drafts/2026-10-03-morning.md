# GLP-1s and Hair Loss in Men: What This Study Actually Says

You've probably seen the headlines today, "New study suggests possible connection between GLP-1s and hair loss in men." If you lift, you already have a hair paranoia meter, and GLP-1 + hair loss sets it off instantly. So let's talk about it, and let's be honest about what we actually know versus what a headline is telling us.

WHAT'S BEING REPORTED

The coverage (Washington Post, and picked up elsewhere) describes a study exploring a GENETIC link between GLP-1 drugs and male pattern hair loss. That's the part I want you to lock onto, because "genetic link" is a very different claim than "semaglutide made guys go bald."

I'm not going to quote effect sizes or percentages here, because the headlines alone don't give them and I'm not inventing numbers for you. What I can do is explain the two competing explanations that any study like this has to untangle, because that's where the whole thing lives or dies.

EXPLANATION 1: IT'S THE WEIGHT LOSS, NOT THE DRUG

This is called telogen effluvium, and it's the most boring, most likely answer.

Your hair follicles cycle through a growth phase and a resting/shedding phase. When your body goes through a big stressor, rapid weight loss, a huge calorie deficit, low protein intake, low iron, surgery, illness, a chunk of follicles get kicked out of growth and into shed mode all at once. Then a few months later, you're pulling hair out of the shower drain in handfuls.

Think of it like your body triaging. Hair is NOT an essential organ. When resources get tight fast, it's one of the first things to get defunded.

This is extremely well documented after bariatric surgery, and GLP-1s at high doses produce bariatric-sized weight loss with bariatric-sized drops in food intake. Shedding on a GLP-1 was always going to happen to some people regardless of the molecule. The key detail with telogen effluvium is that it's usually TEMPORARY and it usually grows back once intake and weight stabilize.

EXPLANATION 2: IT'S ANDROGENETIC ALOPECIA

Male pattern baldness is a completely different beast. It's genetic, it's DHT-driven, it's progressive, and it does NOT just grow back. It's the same pathway you guys already worry about on AAS.

That's why a genetic angle on this is actually interesting. If researchers are looking at genetic predisposition alongside GLP-1 exposure, the question they're chasing is basically: are these drugs unmasking or accelerating pattern loss in men who were already genetically loaded for it, rather than causing a generic shed in everybody?

That's a real question. I don't have the full numbers to answer it for you, and I'd rather tell you that than make something up.

WHAT WE STILL DON'T KNOW

* Is it the drug, the deficit, or the genetics? These three are tangled together in every single person taking a GLP-1.
* Is it reversible? Telogen effluvium is. Androgenetic alopecia isn't. Huge difference in what it means for you.
* Is it dose-dependent or rate-dependent? Nobody has cleanly separated "lost 20% of bodyweight fast" from "took semaglutide."
* Does it apply to tirzepatide or reta? Almost all of this signal work starts with semaglutide first.

WHAT I'D ACTUALLY PAY ATTENTION TO

1. Protein. If you're eating 60g a day because you're never hungry, hair is going to be one of the first things to complain. Hit your protein target even when appetite is gone.
2. Micronutrients. Iron, ferritin, zinc, vitamin D, B12. Bloodwork tells you this in 10 minutes. Low intake for months tells you nothing until the shed starts.
3. Rate of loss. Slower, steadier loss stresses the system less than crashing. This is also the same advice for muscle retention, so it's free.
4. Timeline. Telogen effluvium shows up roughly 2-4 months AFTER the stressor, not during it. People constantly blame the wrong week.
5. If it's thinning at the temples and crown in a pattern, that's a dermatologist conversation, not a GLP-1 conversation.

ALSO ON MY RADAR THIS WEEK

There's separate reporting out of EADV 2026 on GLP-1 nail disorders being more common in a multinational study. Same category of thing, nails and hair are both fast-dividing tissue that reflect your nutritional status first. I want to see that data in full before I say anything about magnitude.

Bottom line: a possible connection is not a proven cause, and "genetic link" is a research question, not a verdict. If you're on a GLP-1 and shedding, the most likely culprit is still how fast you're dropping weight and what you're eating while you do it. That part you control.
