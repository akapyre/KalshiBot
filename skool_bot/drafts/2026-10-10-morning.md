# What I'm Watching This Week: Anti-Aging GLP-1 Headlines, A Cheek Patch, and Tirzepatide In Psoriasis

The news cycle this week was mostly noise. A pile of "best weight loss drug 2026" SEO articles, a bunch of stock takes on Novo, and supplier press releases. None of that is worth a full breakdown, so let's talk about the three things that actually caught my eye, and be very clear about what we DON'T know yet from a headline alone.

1. "GLP-1s May Turn Back The Clock"

There's coverage going around saying the anti-aging exploration around tirzepatide and semaglutide is heating up. This is the one you guys are going to see clipped on TikTok within a week, so get ahead of it.

Here's the honest framing: this is a REPORT ABOUT INTEREST, not a trial result. I can't tell you an effect size, a population, or a duration from a headline, and neither can anybody reposting it. What's biologically plausible is that a lot of the things GLP-1s already do (weight loss, lower inflammatory load, better glycemic control, better blood pressure, less visceral fat) are the same things that move aging-related biomarkers. That's not the same as the drug being geroprotective on its own.

The question I want answered: when the actual papers drop, is it an epigenetic clock readout in a small cohort, or hard outcomes? Those are VERY different claims. I'll break it down when there's a study attached instead of a press cycle.

2. A Needle-Free GLP-1 That Sticks To Your Cheek

A company called Insu is working on buccal (cheek-adhering) delivery for insulin and GLP-1s. This is the part of the space I find genuinely underrated.

The problem has always been the same: peptides are chains of amino acids, and your gut treats them like food. Stomach acid and digestive enzymes chew them up before they ever reach the bloodstream, which is why most of this category is injectable. Oral semaglutide only works because it's paired with an absorption enhancer and you still lose most of the dose.

Buccal delivery tries to skip that entirely by absorbing through the tissue inside your cheek, straight into circulation, bypassing first-pass liver metabolism. Cool idea. The hard part is always bioavailability and consistency. I have no human data here, just a development announcement, so treat it as "a thing to watch," NOT a thing that exists.

3. Tirzepatide Showing Up In Psoriasis Research

This one is the most gym-relevant in a sneaky way. There's new coverage on ixekizumab and tirzepatide broadening biomarker effects in psoriasis and obesity.

Why that matters: psoriasis is an inflammatory condition, and obesity drives systemic inflammation. If tirzepatide is moving inflammatory biomarkers in that population, it supports the idea that these drugs aren't just appetite tools, they're metabolic and inflammatory levers. We've already seen that story play out with cardiovascular, kidney and sleep apnea outcomes.

Again, I'm not quoting you numbers I don't have. Biomarker movement is not the same as symptom resolution, and a lot of it could simply be downstream of losing fat mass.

THE THEME

All three of these are early. One is a press cycle, one is a prototype, one is biomarker data. None of them change what you're doing today. The reason I still post them is because this is how you learn to spot the difference between a headline and a finding BEFORE someone tries to sell you something off of it.
