# Myth vs. Fact: "GLP-1s Cure Addiction"

You've probably seen the clips. Somebody gets on sema or tirz for weight, and three weeks later they're saying they quit drinking, stopped vaping, stopped doom-buying stuff on Amazon at 1am. "The food noise went away AND every other noise went away too." It's all over social right now, and there are already clinics marketing GLP-1s for addiction. So let's talk about it.

THE CLAIM

"GLP-1s cure addiction. Alcohol, nicotine, shopping, scrolling, all of it."

THE GRADE: PARTIALLY TRUE, BUT THE MARKETING IS WAY AHEAD OF THE DATA

There's a real mechanism here. That part is not hype. What IS hype is the word "cure," and the idea that this is settled.

WHY THERE'S SOMETHING HERE

GLP-1 receptors are NOT just in your gut and pancreas. They show up in the brainstem and in the reward circuitry, the same areas that light up when you eat, drink, or hit a slot machine. GLP-1 signaling appears to turn down how loud the "I want that" signal gets.

Think of it like a volume knob, not a mute button. The craving isn't deleted. It just stops screaming at you from across the room. That's the same reason people describe food noise going quiet, and it would make sense that a shared reward pathway doesn't care whether the reward is a donut or a drink.

WHAT THE EVIDENCE ACTUALLY LOOKS LIKE

1. Animal work is the strongest leg. Rodent studies have been pointing at reduced intake of rewarding substances for years, and that's what got researchers interested in the first place.

2. Human data is early. We're talking small trials and observational signals pulled from health records, not big, long, randomized trials built specifically to treat addiction.

3. A LOT of what you're seeing online is self-report. "I just stopped wanting it" is a real observation, but it's an anecdote, and anecdotes don't tell you how often it happens or who it fails for.

4. No GLP-1 is FDA-approved for any addiction indication. Not one. Everything in that lane right now is off-label or investigational.

WHY CRITICS ARE PUSHING BACK

There's reporting out this week on exactly this: clinics and marketers promoting GLP-1s for addiction while the science is still in its early innings. The criticism is fair and worth hearing:

* Addiction is behavioral, social, and environmental, not just one receptor misbehaving.
* Appetite suppression is easy to measure. "Reduced craving" is messy and heavily influenced by expectation.
* Nobody can tell you what happens after you stop, which is the same open question we have with weight regain.

WHAT WOULD MOVE THIS FROM "MAYBE" TO "YES"

* Larger randomized trials with addiction as the actual endpoint, not a side observation
* Longer follow-up, because relapse is a long-game problem
* Data on what happens when the drug comes off

MY VERDICT

Mechanism: plausible and genuinely exciting. Animal data: real. Human data: early and thin. The claim that GLP-1s "cure" addiction: NOT supported yet. The honest version is "GLP-1s may turn down reward signaling, and researchers are actively testing whether that helps, with early results interesting enough to keep studying."

That's a much less fun TikTok, but it's the truth, and if this pans out it's going to be one of the biggest things to come out of this drug class.
