# Tirzepatide vs Semaglutide: What an "Indirect Comparison" Actually Tells You

New paper dropped in Nature today comparing tirzepatide and semaglutide for obesity/overweight in people WITHOUT type 2 diabetes. This is the exact question I get asked more than almost anything else, so let's talk about it, and more importantly, let's talk about what this kind of study can and can't answer.

First, quick refresher on the two compounds.

* Semaglutide is a single agonist. It hits the GLP-1 receptor only.
* Tirzepatide is a dual agonist. It hits GLP-1 AND GIP.

That second receptor is the whole story. GLP-1 slows gastric emptying, boosts glucose-dependent insulin release, and signals satiety in the hypothalamus. GIP is the other big incretin hormone, and adding it appears to change how fat tissue handles nutrients and may soften some of the nausea signaling. Two faucets instead of one.

WHAT "INDIRECT COMPARISON" MEANS

This is the part people skip, and it's the most important part of the paper.

An indirect comparison does NOT mean they put people in a room and gave half of them one drug and half the other. It means researchers took trials that were run separately, usually each compound vs. placebo, and used statistical modeling to bridge them through that shared placebo arm.

Think of it like comparing two lifters who never competed against each other, but both beat the same third guy. You can make an educated guess about who's stronger. You cannot call it a result.

1. Different trials have different populations: age, starting BMI, sex split, baseline weight.
2. Different trials have different lengths and different titration schedules.
3. Different trials define and report side effects slightly differently.
4. Dropout rates vary, and how you handle dropouts can swing a weight-loss number by a lot.

That's why indirect comparisons sit BELOW a real head-to-head trial on the evidence ladder. They're useful, they're published in serious journals, and payers and health systems genuinely use them. But they are modeling, not measurement.

WHAT I WANT YOU GUYS TO TAKE FROM THIS

The "which one is stronger" debate gets treated like a settled fact on social media, and it isn't. Anyone quoting you a clean percentage difference between these two compounds should be able to tell you whether that number came from a direct trial or a statistical bridge. Most people can't, because they read a headline and not the methods section.

Also worth noting: this paper is specifically in people WITHOUT type 2 diabetes. That matters. Non-diabetic populations generally lose more weight on incretin drugs than diabetic populations do, so you can't take numbers from one group and apply them to the other.
